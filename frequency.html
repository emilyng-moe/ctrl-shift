01

From Work Problems to Working Prototypes

Work problem

Prototype

↻ hover to replay

What happens when officers are given time to step away from day-to-day work and focus on one real problem with AI? At the AI-First Accelerator Community Day on 24 August, 261 officers did just that, creating 47 AI-assisted solutions across areas such as staffing, audit planning, email checking, invoicing, and subsidy monitoring.

The day was designed to move teams beyond talking about AI and into building with it. Officers worked through real work challenges, sharpened their problem statements, and developed early prototypes that could be tested and improved.

One lesson stood out: the strongest ideas started with a clear understanding of the work problem, not with the AI tool. Teams that were able to define the problem well could move more quickly into designing and testing possible solutions.

Seven top-voted teams are now receiving further support to strengthen their prototypes ahead of the Gallery Walk on 30 September. For everyone else, the takeaway is just as relevant: before asking what AI can do, start by asking which part of your work is worth rethinking.

What happens when HQ and school officers come together to tackle real school challenges, with just 10 minutes and AI at their side?

At the Prompt Royale x MOE Finals on 11 September, five finalist duos took on three challenges drawn from real school contexts. They had to make sense of the problem quickly, decide what the AI needed to know, test their approach, and refine it on the spot, using only four approved tools: Pair, Desk, AISAY, and Opus.

What made the finals especially interesting was the mix of perspectives in the room. HQ officers brought one view of the system, while school officers brought the realities of day-to-day practice. Together, they showed how AI can become more useful when different experiences are brought to the same problem.

And if 10 minutes, four tools, and a clear challenge were enough to surface useful ideas and new approaches, it raises a bigger question: what could we achieve if teams had more time to explore, test, and build around the challenges that matter most?

Kenny and Wee Lin emerged as this year's champions, with Ee Jun and Frederick taking first runners-up, and Eugene and Jin Wee placing second runners-up. But the bigger takeaway from the finals was not just who won. It was what became possible when real problems, different perspectives, and AI came together.

03

What Could One Hour a Week Change?

One hour a week. Twelve weeks running.

↻ hover to replay

AI doesn't have to be something you cram in all at once. The 12-Week Learning Plan asks for just one hour a week — and builds learning straight into how you already work.

AI can feel like something you need to catch up on overnight. The 12-Week Learning Plan takes the opposite approach: start small, stay consistent, and learn through the work you're already doing.

Teams set aside just one hour a week to learn, experiment, and apply AI to a real work challenge — no separate course, no one-off workshop, just learning built into the rhythm of work.

Over 12 weeks, teams move from understanding the problem and using AI responsibly, to testing it on real tasks and rethinking the wider workflow. The goal isn't to know more about AI — it's to have improved one piece of work, and found a habit worth keeping.

Start with one problem. Give it one hour a week. See where the small changes add up. The plan is simple — the harder part is just making the space to begin.

01

AI-First Gallery Walk: What Three Weeks of Refinement Can Do

⤢

Just a few weeks ago, 260 officers came together at AI First Community Day and built 47 AI-assisted solutions around real work challenges. For eight teams across six divisions, that was only the starting point.

Over the next three weeks, they continued testing their ideas, gathering feedback and refining their prototypes. At the AI First Gallery Walk, they returned to share how their solutions had evolved with more than 120 colleagues.

What stood out was not simply how much the prototypes had changed, but how quickly teams had moved through the cycle of identifying a problem, building something, testing it, learning from feedback and improving it.

Some assumptions changed. Features were adjusted. Ideas that seemed promising on paper had to be tested against the realities of the work itself. In the process, early concepts became more tangible solutions.

It was also encouraging to see strong support from MOE’s leadership team. Senior management and Division Directors came by to understand the ideas firsthand, ask questions and share feedback with the teams. Their engagement helped make the Gallery Walk more than a showcase. It became a space for teams to test their thinking, hear different perspectives and consider how their ideas might develop further.

The conversations extended beyond the teams themselves. Colleagues explored how the solutions could apply in other contexts, exchanged ideas and discussed where AI could genuinely improve the way we work.

One reflection shared during the session, including by 2PS, was how quickly an idea can move forward when officers have the opportunity to experiment and learn by doing.

That is also a useful reminder for any team exploring AI: you do not need to have a fully formed solution before you begin. Start with a real problem, build something small, put it in front of people and use what you learn to make the next version better.

From 260 officers → 47 initial solutions → 8 teams → three weeks of testing and refinement, the Gallery Walk showed how quickly ideas can develop when there is space to test, learn and iterate.

02

Rethink the Work: Turn a Work Problem into an AI-Powered Solution

Tap a step to explore

Sometimes the best place to start with AI is not by asking, “What can this tool do?” but “What could we do differently?”

Think about a piece of work your team regularly deals with. Perhaps information has to be gathered from multiple sources, a repetitive process still involves several manual steps, or colleagues spend time sorting and checking information before getting to the part that actually requires judgement.

Instead of using AI to make one step faster, what if you stepped back and reconsidered the workflow itself?

That is the idea behind AI Build 301, a learning pathway from the Institute of Digital Government for public officers who want to move beyond using AI for individual tasks and start designing and prototyping AI-powered solutions at work.

The pathway starts with the problem, not the technology.

1. Frame the problem

Before deciding what to build, get clear on what is actually getting in the way. Who experiences the problem? What happens today? Where does time or effort go? What would a better outcome look like?

For an MOE team, that might mean moving from: “Can we build an AI tool to analyse feedback?” to: “We receive hundreds of open-ended responses, and officers spend hours sorting them before they can identify recurring concerns. How might we surface the important themes earlier while keeping human judgement in the process?”

The second question gives you much more to work with.

2. Build something small

Once the problem is clearer, the next step is to turn the idea into something you can test. That does not necessarily mean building a full application. It could be a simple workflow, chatbot, AI skill or prototype that helps you see whether the idea works.

The aim is not to get it right on the first try. It is to make the idea tangible enough to test, learn from and improve.

3. Check that it actually improves the work

A technically impressive prototype is not necessarily a useful one. The real question is whether it solves the problem you started with and makes the work better in practice.

So this month, try looking beyond the individual task you want AI to speed up. Where does the work get stuck? What are people still doing manually because “that’s how we’ve always done it”? Which parts could be redesigned rather than simply automated?

Pick one problem. Frame it clearly. Build the smallest version of a different way of working. Then test it. That is often where rethinking the work begins.

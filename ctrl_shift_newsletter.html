This month's spotlight

Drafting faster without losing your voice

One of the most common frustrations with AI-generated text: it sounds like a robot wrote it. Here's how one team cracked it.

⚡

The challenge

The team needed to produce weekly stakeholder updates — but the drafts always felt generic and required heavy editing before they sounded like the team's actual voice.

🔬

What they tried

They gave the AI three examples of past updates they were happy with, then asked it to match that tone and structure when drafting new ones. They also added a "house rules" section to their prompt — things like "avoid corporate jargon" and "keep it under 200 words".

✅

What happened

First drafts went from needing 30 minutes of editing to about 5. The team now spends more time on substance — what to say — rather than how to say it.

Try it yourself

Paste 2–3 examples of writing you're proud of into your next prompt. Tell the AI: "Match the tone and style of these examples." Then give it your raw notes to work with.

💡 You don't need to be technical. You just need to be curious.

Tool of the Month: AIAssistant Desk
Your AI workspace — built for government work

AIAssistant Desk is MOE's approved AI workspace. Unlike a basic chatbot, it lets you work with uploaded files, search the web, and remember your preferences across sessions — all within the approved government boundary.

  • Upload documents and ask questions directly about their content
  • Draft, summarise, and rewrite — with context from your actual files
  • Use specialised assistants for different tasks (writing, analysis, research)
  • Works at Restricted / Sensitive Normal classification level

⚡ Quick tip

When you're stuck on how to start a prompt, try this structure:

You are a [role]. I need you to [task]. Here is the context: [paste your notes]. Output format: [bullet points / table / paragraph].

This works for almost any task — drafting, summarising, analysing, or planning.

What they did

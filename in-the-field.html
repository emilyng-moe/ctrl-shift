Customer Lens - How an Idea Evolved Through Experimentation

At AI First Community Day in August, officers from across MOE came together to explore how AI could help solve real work challenges. Among them was a team from SPSD’s Customer Services Branch (CSB) and DXD, who started exploring how customer insights could be used more meaningfully in everyday work.

Their early idea became Customer Lens, one of eight prototypes that continued on to the AI First Gallery Walk at the end of September. Over the weeks in between, the team tested, refined and developed the idea further, turning an initial concept into a more considered prototype.

From customer data to a working prototype

The team brought together different areas of expertise, from data analysis and user research to process innovation, product design and technical development.

“What united us was a shared interest in using customer insights more meaningfully in our work and making it easier for others to do so.”

CSB already has a wealth of customer insights, but these are spread across user research studies, CRM case records, call transcripts and different operational systems. For officers, piecing these together to understand the broader customer picture can take considerable time.

The team initially imagined an AI-powered chatbot that could make this information easier to access. As they developed the idea, they experimented with low/no-code and vibe-coding tools to move beyond a basic chatbot and create a more user-friendly experience around the AI model.

Over time, the purpose of Customer Lens evolved too. It became less about simply retrieving customer information and more about helping officers make sense of customer evidence and apply it to their work.

Building around how officers actually work

To understand what that could look like in practice, the team engaged five potential user groups across MOE to learn how they currently use customer insights and where they encounter gaps.

They found that different users wanted to use customer evidence in different ways. Some wanted to understand sentiment around a topic. Others wanted to brainstorm possible responses to an issue, while some wanted to stress-test an existing proposal.

That shaped a key decision in the prototype: rather than build a one-size-fits-all tool, the team created three modes in Customer Lens: Exploratory, Brainstorming and Solutioning.

“We saw a need for the tool to be flexible in meeting the varied needs of these potential users.”

Testing changed the solution

As the team tested the prototype with realistic scenarios, they uncovered another important challenge: how to make AI-generated insights useful while keeping the distinction between evidence and inference clear. Some insights generated by the AI were not directly derived from the customer data provided, but inferred by the model.

The team responded by refining the prompts and guardrails, making the distinction between direct customer evidence and AI-inferred insights clearer. They also introduced an “Areas to validate further” section, so gaps or uncertainty in the evidence could be surfaced rather than hidden.

“Uncertainty or gaps in the available evidence could be surfaced rather than hidden.”

This became an important part of the team’s learning. A useful AI solution is not simply one that generates an answer. It also needs to help users understand what is supported by evidence, what has been inferred, and where further validation is needed.

More than building the AI

The team found that developing Customer Lens involved much more than building the AI model itself. Considerable effort went into preparing and structuring fragmented data, understanding different user needs, optimising how the data could be used effectively, and designing outputs that officers could interpret responsibly.

The team was learning too.

“The project became a learning-by-doing experience for us as well.”

Their biggest learning was that AI works best here as an aid to human judgement, rather than a replacement for it.

“The value is not simply in generating an answer; it is in helping officers discover relevant customer insights, understand the problem better, challenge assumptions and recognise where further validation is needed.”

What could Customer Lens enable next?

The team sees potential for Customer Lens to become part of how officers approach policy and service design.

An officer developing a new policy, changing a process or preparing a communication could use the tool earlier in the process to understand what existing customer evidence tells us, identify possible friction or knowledge gaps, and consider how a proposed change might land with customers.

There is also potential to expand the range of topics and customer evidence available over time, alongside the appropriate data quality, governance and validation.

For teams thinking about building their own AI solutions, the Customer Lens team’s advice is simple:

“Start with the problem and user needs.”

Understand the existing workflow. Identify where the real pain points are. Test early. Iterate as you learn.

And do not worry about having all the technical expertise from day one. The team’s experience with vibe coding and low/no-code tools showed that you can learn as you build, while staying focused on solving the actual work problem.

From an initial idea to a tangible prototype in just weeks, Customer Lens evolved as the team learnt more about the problem and the people they were designing for. What began as an AI-powered way to retrieve customer insights grew into a tool that helps officers explore evidence, brainstorm possible responses and test policies, processes or communications from the customer perspective.

The journey is a reminder that rethinking the work is often about more than introducing a new tool. It is about looking at how work is currently done, questioning what could work differently, and letting what we learn shape the next version.

Start with the problem. Stay close to the users. Let what you learn shape the solution.

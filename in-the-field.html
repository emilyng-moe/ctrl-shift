💬 In the Field

Stories from MOE officers

Real people, real experiments, real results. No success theatre — just honest sharing.

HQ Officer
"You don't need to wait for the perfect AI use case. Start with one frustrating piece of work, build something small and test it. You might be surprised by where it takes you."

What if AI could do the first pass, so you could focus on what actually needs your judgement? For Isaac SQ Lim, Manager (School Finance Transformation), that question began with a familiar kind of work: reading documents, checking them against requirements, spotting what's missing, and following up when something doesn't add up. He works on transformation projects across MOE Finance, improving everyday processes through redesign, digitalisation and, increasingly, AI — and has also been exploring how AI could support areas such as managing and responding to school finance queries. The AI-First Accelerator Community Day on 24 Aug gave him the chance to move beyond talking about AI and actually build with it, so he picked a common pain point: the time officers spend manually reading and checking documents.

A first pass, not the final say

During the AI-First Accelerator, Isaac developed a prototype to help officers review contractor service reports. A submission might include a service report, photographs, invoices, test results and other supporting documents. Instead of checking every item manually, the AI performs an initial review against the original requirements — identifying what appears to have been completed, what may have been claimed without enough evidence, and what cannot be verified. But the AI does not make the final approval decision. The officer does. Think of it as a first-pass reviewer: AI organises the evidence and flags what deserves attention, while the officer applies the context, accountability and professional judgement needed to make the call.

Building fast is one thing. Building reliably is another.

One of Isaac's biggest surprises was how quickly he could move from an idea to something that worked. Instead of a lengthy cycle of requirements gathering, development and testing, he could experiment, test the output and refine the solution almost immediately. But that speed revealed an important lesson: getting AI to produce a good answer once is very different from getting consistently reliable answers. The harder work was thinking through edge cases, defining what "good" looks like, testing different scenarios and deciding where human involvement is essential — that process was just as important as building the prototype itself.

One pattern, many possibilities

Isaac sees potential well beyond contractor reports. Many processes follow a similar pattern: read, compare, verify and flag exceptions. The documents and rules may change, but the underlying task remains much the same. The same approach could help check whether required information is present, whether supporting documents match what has been declared, or whether a case needs an officer's attention before proceeding. For school finance queries, AI could retrieve relevant guidance and prepare a response for an officer to review.

Looking ahead, Isaac sees AI becoming less like something we occasionally open to ask a question, and more like a natural part of the workflow. Imagine receiving a case where AI has already read the documents, pulled together the important information, checked it against the requirements, and highlighted the three things that actually need your attention. The opportunity is not simply to replace whole processes — it's to remove repetitive work so officers can spend more time on judgement, problem-solving and working with people.

IL
Isaac SQ Lim
Manager, School Finance Transformation · FSSSB

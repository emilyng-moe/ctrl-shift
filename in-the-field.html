AI-First

From AI user to AI builder: Two officers reflect on their AI-First journey

AI-First gave officers the opportunity to move beyond using AI for everyday tasks and explore how it could help them rethink the way work is done. For Jason Wang from Customer Services Branch (CSB) and Justin Loh from Procurement, Finance and School Support (PF&SS), the experience was about turning familiar work challenges into opportunities to experiment, build and improve.

While they started from different experiences with AI, both discovered that meaningful innovation begins with curiosity, a willingness to try and a deeper understanding of the problems they were trying to solve.

Jason Wang: Discovering what he could build with AI

From using AI to building a solution of his own

Before AI-First, Jason was already comfortable using AI tools such as Copilot and Pair to draft emails, review submissions, summarise information and explore ideas. He was also used to refining prompts to get better results.

But building an application from scratch was new territory. Although he had some basic HTML knowledge from his polytechnic days, he had never built a standalone application, much less one powered by AI.

As an AI Champion from CSB’s Contact Centre, Jason wanted to explore how AI could address a real challenge in customer service — quality assurance of customer email replies.

With officers handling a high volume of enquiries, supervisors can only review a sample of replies. The team saw an opportunity for AI to help check replies against approved knowledge sources, flagging those that may be inaccurate, incomplete or require closer attention.

This led to the CSB Email Checker, a prototype designed to validate customer enquiries and officers’ replies in batches, allowing supervisors to focus on flagged cases, complex issues and staff coaching.

“The goal is not to replace the supervisor’s judgement. It is to make their time go further.”

Learning by doing, one error at a time

Building the prototype was a process of trial and error. Jason recalls spending four or five rounds troubleshooting a single issue in Viber Studio, using AI to explain error messages, suggest fixes and help him work through what went wrong.

Not every fix worked, but each attempt helped him understand the problem better. As the team tested the prototype and gathered feedback, the Email Checker evolved — its interface changed, validation logic was refined and new features were added.

“When something that had been broken for a while finally worked, there was a quiet ‘I actually managed to do it’ moment.”

The experience changed how Jason viewed AI. Previously, he saw it mainly as a tool to help him complete tasks more efficiently. AI-First showed him that he could also use it to build something of his own.

Today, when he encounters an idea for a tool or system, his instinct is no longer to assume that it requires a technical expert. Instead, he asks whether he could build a simple version first and see if it works.

His advice to fellow officers? Start small, focus on the problem, and learn as you go.

“You really do not need to know everything before you start. What mattered more than technical knowledge was being clear about the problem I was trying to solve.”

Justin Loh: Rethinking how work gets done

Beyond doing tasks faster

Justin had already been exploring generative AI before AI-First, including developing an AI-powered TRR generator to simplify administrative work. With his experience in procurement operations and data analytics, he was interested in how technology could address real-world challenges.

AI-First gave him the opportunity to go further — not just using AI to complete tasks faster, but exploring how it could simplify entire workflows.

Through his experience in PF&SS, Justin saw opportunities to reduce manual effort and improve existing processes. This led his team to develop Smarter Invoicing, a prototype designed to streamline invoice tracking and approvals.

The solution automates the routing of invoices through the various officers responsible for verification and approval, while allowing queries to be managed within the system.

“Our goal was simple: less time chasing approvals, more time focusing on work that matters.”

Questioning the way things are done

For Justin, developing the prototype was as much about understanding and rethinking the workflow as it was about building the solution. The experience reinforced the importance of questioning existing processes rather than simply digitising them.

AI also made experimentation more accessible. Ideas could be translated into working prototypes through testing and refinement, allowing the team to explore possibilities that might previously have seemed out of reach.

“Sometimes, the best innovations come from people closest to the problem.”

This changed how Justin approached improvement. Instead of asking, “How can AI help me complete this faster?”, he began asking, “Why are we doing it this way in the first place?”

He sees AI as an opportunity not just to improve productivity, but to rethink workflows and develop practical solutions that benefit colleagues beyond his immediate team.

His curiosity about AI extends beyond work too. He has been exploring agentic AI workflows for cycling, using AI to analyse performance data and support his fitness journey — another example of how he applies experimentation and AI-assisted insights in everyday life.

For officers curious about AI, Justin’s advice is to start with something in their daily work that could be improved. Technical expertise is not a prerequisite; understanding the problem and being willing to experiment are just as valuable.

Starting with a problem, discovering new possibilities

Jason and Justin set out to solve different challenges, but both came away with a shift in perspective. Jason discovered that he could build a solution despite not having a technical background, while Justin learnt to look beyond individual tasks and rethink the processes behind them.

Their experiences show that AI-First is not just about learning to use AI or building a prototype. It is about questioning familiar ways of working, starting with problems we understand, and being willing to experiment with new possibilities.

Sometimes, the first step towards innovation is simply asking: Could we do this differently?

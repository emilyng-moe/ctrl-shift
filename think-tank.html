Use Case of the Month

Don’t Just Automate the Task. Rethink the Workflow.

AI is often used to make an existing task faster. But sometimes the bigger opportunity is to step back and ask whether the workflow itself could be designed differently.

For this month’s Think Tank, we look at Smarter Invoicing, one of the prototypes developed through AI First. The team started with a familiar work problem and explored how AI could take on more of the routine coordination, while keeping officers focused on the parts that require judgement and accountability.

1. The Challenge

Processing an invoice can involve far more than checking the invoice itself.

Officers may need to extract information, contact Project Managers, gather supporting documents, check whether everything is complete, follow up on missing items and route the case through different stages for verification and approval.

The work is not necessarily complex at every step. But there are many handoffs, checks and follow-ups that take time.

That was the challenge the team set out to tackle with Smarter Invoicing.

2. How the Team Rethought the Workflow

Instead of asking, “How can AI help us check invoices faster?”, the team looked at the full process and asked which parts truly needed an officer.

They identified routine steps that could potentially be handled by AI, such as:

  • extracting key information from an e-Invoice
  • generating and sending verification requests
  • collecting supporting documents
  • checking whether submissions are complete
  • flagging exceptions
  • routing the case to the next officer
  • sending reminders for overdue responses

The idea was to let the system handle more of the coordination, while bringing officers in where judgement and accountability were still needed.

3. What Changed

The shift was not simply from manual invoice checking to AI-assisted invoice checking. It was from:

FromOfficers managing every handoff, check and follow-up
↓
ToAI coordinating routine steps, surfacing exceptions and bringing officers in when a decision is needed

Sometimes the opportunity is not to automate one task. It is to redesign the workflow so officers spend less time moving information around and more time on the parts that require judgement.

4. Try This With Your Own Work

Pick one recurring workflow your team manages and map it from start to finish.

1

Map the workflow

What happens from the moment the work starts until it is completed?

2

Spot the friction

Where are people repeatedly copying information, checking documents, routing items or chasing responses?

3

Separate routine from judgement

Which steps genuinely need an officer’s expertise or decision? Which are mainly administrative?

4

Ask what AI could handle

Could AI help extract, summarise, check, route, remind or flag exceptions?

5

Redesign and test

Sketch a simpler workflow, then test one small part before trying to automate the whole process.

The question to start with

Which parts of this workflow really need a person, and which parts could work differently?

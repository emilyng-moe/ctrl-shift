Use Case of the Month

Spreadsheet to Story: Analysing Data with ChatGPT

You've got a spreadsheet full of data, but turning it into something decision-ready takes time — what to analyse, which patterns actually matter, what goes into the PivotTable, and how to turn findings into something useful for a meeting. Instead of figuring it out from scratch, try using ChatGPT as your data analysis partner, all the way from first look to what to do next.

1

Drop in your spreadsheet

Upload your Excel or CSV file and say what you're trying to understand, not just what's in it. Prompt: "I want to understand participation in this programme. Review the dataset, explain what each column contains, flag missing values or inconsistencies, and suggest five useful questions the data could help me answer." This catches issues early and gives you a clearer starting point.

2

Ask what stands out

Instead of deciding upfront what to analyse, let ChatGPT explore first. Prompt: "Analyse this dataset and identify the most significant trends, differences and outliers. Break down the results by relevant groups or time periods, and tell me what deserves a closer look." Then interrogate it further — which groups show the biggest differences, what's driving the overall trend, does the data support your initial assumption.

3

Build the PivotTable with it

Once you know what to investigate, let it work out the structure instead of experimenting manually. Prompt: "I want to compare participation rates across divisions and see how they changed by month. Recommend the PivotTable structure — Rows, Columns, Values and Filters — and explain why." It can also help with calculated fields, grouping or percentages when a standard PivotTable isn't enough.

4

Turn the analysis into a dashboard

Ask ChatGPT to decide what's worth showing, rather than putting every metric on the page. Prompt: "Design a one-page dashboard for senior management. Recommend 3–4 headline metrics and the most useful charts. For each visual, explain the question it answers and the key takeaway." From there, it can help create the charts and write insight-led titles.

5

Turn the dashboard into decisions

A dashboard tells you what's happening — the next step is deciding what deserves attention. Prompt: "Based only on what the data supports, identify the three findings that matter most. For each, explain what the data shows, why it matters, what we still don't know, and what we should investigate or consider doing next." Ask it to turn that into a management brief, talking points, or slide headlines.

Try it end to end

"Act as my data analysis partner. Review the spreadsheet I've uploaded and help me understand what matters. First, explain the dataset and flag any data quality issues. Suggest useful questions to investigate, then identify significant trends, differences and outliers. Recommend the PivotTables or calculations needed to investigate them further. Based on the findings, propose a one-page dashboard with the right KPIs and charts. Finally, identify the three most important findings — why they matter, what we still don't know, and what to investigate or consider next. Clearly distinguish between what the data shows and your interpretation."
